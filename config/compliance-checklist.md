# Compliance checklist — infant formula content (Gate 4 companion)

Infant formula marketing is regulated under the WHO International Code of
Marketing of Breast-milk Substitutes and national law in many markets. The
human reviewer checks each draft against this list alongside medical accuracy.
This checklist is a working aid, not legal advice — have local counsel review
it per target market.

## Every article

- [ ] Does not idealize formula feeding or present it as equivalent/superior to breastfeeding
- [ ] Where the article discusses feeding choices, acknowledges breastfeeding guidance (WHO: exclusive breastfeeding to 6 months) neutrally and factually
- [ ] No health or nutrition claims prohibited in the target market
- [ ] No language that discourages breastfeeding or undermines a caregiver's confidence in it
- [ ] Medical claims match the fact sheet exactly (no drift introduced after Gate 3)
- [ ] Distinguishes clearly between infant formula (0–12 mo) and follow-on/toddler products where regulation differs
- [ ] No implied endorsement by health authorities ("WHO-approved", "pediatrician recommended") unless literally true and citable
- [ ] Tone is informational, not promotional, when a specific product category is named

## Site-level (checked once, re-verified quarterly)

- [ ] Author bio pages with real credentials exist and are linked
- [ ] Medical reviewer byline (name, license) appears on every reviewed article
- [ ] "Medically reviewed on <date>" is accurate
- [ ] Editorial / review policy page is published

## Escalation

Any draft where compliance is ambiguous is HELD — it does not ship with notes,
it waits for a definitive answer.
